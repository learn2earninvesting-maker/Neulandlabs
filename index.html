The big picture

What the report concludes, in one screen.

Growth has rebounded sharply after FY25, led by commercial custom manufacturing (making ingredients for drug companies' already-approved medicines) and strong margins. The counterweight: a small group of molecules drives much of that revenue, quarterly results swing a lot, and the company is entering a capital-intensive expansion.

Tailwinds

  • Revenue up 37.0% in FY26 to ₹2,023 cr; margin recovered to 28.4%.
  • Q1 FY27 revenue ₹642 cr, up 119.2% on the same quarter last year.
  • First commercial peptide module is now running (7,000 L).
  • One more commercial molecule expected in FY27, one or two in FY28.
  • Money tied up in day-to-day operations fell to 84 days of sales from 137 (working-capital days).

Watch-outs

  • About three commercial molecules carry most custom-manufacturing revenue.
  • Orders don't arrive every quarter — Q1 FY26 margin was 11.8%, Q4 FY26 was 39.6%.
  • FY26 spending on plants (capex, ₹398 cr) exceeded cash generated; free cash flow was −₹50 cr.
  • Management warns return on capital (ROCE) may dip during expansion.
  • Valuation leaves shares sensitive to continued delivery.
"Q1FY27 is broadly in line with our expectations and represents a good start to the year."Saharsh Davuluri, CEO & MD · Aug 5, 2026

Management called Q1 a good start — not a new baseline. It declined to give a precise annual revenue forecast; its growth aspiration is about 20% for FY27 and FY28.

Two engines

What Neuland sells, and which part drives today's earnings.

GDSGeneric Drug Substances

Neuland makes the active ingredients (APIs) that generic-drug companies put into their medicines — it does not make finished medicines.

  • Prime APIs — mature products, more exposed to competition.
  • Specialty APIs — more complex, can be higher value.

Role: gives breadth to the business.

CMSCustom Manufacturing Solutions

Neuland develops and manufactures ingredients for innovator (original-drug) companies, from clinical trials through to commercial supply.

  • Development work can earn revenue before a drug is approved.
  • Commercial supply can recur once the customer's drug is on the market.

Role: the largest growth driver. Management said more than half of revenue came from CMS/CDMO in Q2 FY26.

Pipeline conversion

One more commercial launch expected in FY27; one or possibly two in FY28. These are expectations, not realised revenue.

New development work

Two fairly advanced clinical projects entered in FY27 — management stressed they are not commercial. FY27 revenue from them was not quantified.

Lumpiness

Orders don't necessarily arrive every quarter and customer timing can shift. Management says current commercial performance is not a one-off.

Free cash flow vs net debt (₹ cr)

Net debt below zero means the company holds more cash than borrowings.

Growth reset

Revenue went from ₹951 cr (FY22) to ₹1,559 cr (FY24), fell 5.2% in FY25, then grew 37.0% in FY26. Margin followed: 29.7% → 21.9% → 28.4%.

Cash & investment

Net debt in FY22–23 turned to net cash from FY24. In FY26, capex of ₹398 cr pushed free cash flow to −₹50 cr as investment stepped up.

Returns (ROCE)

Return on capital was 30.1% in FY24, 21.1% in FY25 and 23.6% in FY26. New plants may add capital before they run at full use.

Call figures differ

The Q1 FY27 call reported total income ₹650.1 cr, EBITDA ₹231.1 cr and PAT ₹147.4 cr. These are a different reported series from the table (revenue ₹642 cr, EBITDA ₹249 cr) — don't swap one into the other.

Why margins were high

Management credited spreading fixed costs over more sales (operating leverage), customer mix and partly currency. Long-term target stays 25%+; anything above is a "bonus", not a new floor.

Working capital

Improved to 84 days from 137 at FY26 end, with a stated focus on turning profit into cash. Given the quarterly swings, still worth tracking.

Growth plans & new capacity

From the latest concall and September 2026 update.

Management's ~20% growth aspiration for FY27 and FY28 is an aspiration, not a firm forecast. A CDMO (contract developer and manufacturer) typically sees about six months of orders ahead.

Order book

The CMS order book was described as encouraging, with projects playing out over coming quarters. Customer ordering patterns can still change shipment timing.

Development → commercial

Development revenue is expected to rise in FY27 and feed commercial revenue from FY28. This is an expectation, not a confirmed schedule for each project.

Peptide capacity — coming online in stages

OperationalModule 1 · 7,000 L

First of four planned commercial peptide modules started operations (Sept 2026 update). Qualification, customer production and utilisation still need to progress. Customer interest is converting into better visibility on its use.

PlannedModule 2 · up to 18,000 L

Being configured together with a customer. Further investment will follow customer requirements — no firm start date given.

Beyond obesity drugs: oncology is another early-stage peptide area. Neuland's first peptide API milestone was a FY25 US DMF filing (a regulatory dossier) for Difelikefalin.

Other expansion

Unit III ramp-up

New blocks completed in FY25. By Q3 FY26 ramp-up had started and volumes shipped, but utilisation was still early.

Unit I capacity

Q1 FY27 approved ₹203 cr of investment, ₹196 cr for strategic growth — mostly Unit I. The next phase will be larger, aimed at newer, more complex modalities.

Gland Pharma tie-up

August 2026 sterile-API collaboration — an asset-light entry using Gland's sterile facilities rather than building its own. Scale and contribution not yet shown.

Risk & catalyst scorecard

For each area: what could go right, and what has to be proven.

Commercial CMS

Catalyst
One expected launch in FY27 and one or two in FY28 could broaden the commercial base.
Risk / proof point
A handful of molecules still drive most commercial CMS revenue; shipments can be uneven by quarter.

Peptides

Catalyst
Module 1 is running, customer interest is improving utilisation visibility, and Module 2 is being configured with a customer.
Risk / proof point
Being operational doesn't prove utilisation or returns; further capacity depends on execution and customer needs.

New capacity

Catalyst
Unit III ramp-up and Unit I expansion could support more production and growth.
Risk / proof point
Capex is rising; execution, utilisation and long lead times affect returns. Management cautioned ROCE may dip during expansion.

Gland collaboration

Catalyst
Asset-light entry into sterile API manufacturing may broaden the product portfolio.
Risk / proof point
Scale, timing and economics of future products are not yet established.

GDS portfolio

Catalyst
Specialty APIs, new filings and products such as Ezetimibe offer growth beyond CMS.
Risk / proof point
Mature Prime APIs face more competition; Specialty performance can hinge on individual molecules and markets.

Cash conversion

Catalyst
Q1 FY27 working-capital days improved to 84, supporting the cash-conversion focus.
Risk / proof point
Export exposure, inventory and delivery timing can hit cash flow; Q2 FY26 working capital was 155 days of sales.
